Gifting Page Handover

Why the page is built the way it is, routed through who's actually landing on it.

saakshi-m11.github.io/Mia-Gifting-Page

The short version: a person buying jewellery as a gift is deciding on someone else's behalf, often blind on size and taste, so this page's job is to remove that uncertainty rather than just display product. Six visitor types, from someone buying last-minute to someone shopping weeks ahead to someone buying for themselves, each get a specific, evidenced answer to what would otherwise stop them from completing a purchase. Every section can be traced back to Mia's own order data, a recorded customer complaint, or an evidenced gifting behaviour, not a stylistic guess. Everything below is organised by where it sits in the actual buying journey and collapsed by default, expand any persona or section for the full reasoning behind it.

01Visual & Creative Direction

Our call

The product photography deliberately uses clean, minimal backgrounds instead of busy or colourful product backdrops. Competitor cards in this category often use decorative or coloured backgrounds, which reads closer to mass market. Clean backgrounds keep focus on the piece itself and match the "considered high value purchase" positioning the whole page is built around.

02Who's Actually Landing On This Page

Six visitor types, built from Mia's own order data, search data and complaint records, plus documented Indian jewellery gifting behaviour where Mia's own data doesn't cover it. Click a name to expand.

The Anxious Gifter ▾

What they need to see: a way to buy blind, on size, on taste, without it becoming a costly mistake if they get it wrong.

This is the default emotional state for anyone buying jewellery for someone else, not an edge case. Sizing and exchange difficulty are Mia's own two most recurring recorded complaints, and gift giving anxiety research (Wooten, 2000) shows this anxiety peaks specifically with close relatives, the exact recipient profile for most jewellery gifts.

The Late Planner ▾

What they need to see: speed over choice. They've run out of time to deliberate and need something that will definitely arrive in time.

Gold and silver coin are Mia's #1 and #2 site search terms, ahead of rings, the clearest evidence that a real share of visitors want the zero decision, fastest delivery option, not more curation.

The Early, Occasion-Aware Planner ▾

What they need to see: confidence that if they order now, it will land on the exact right day, not just "within a few days."

Mia's own order data shows purchases peaking 8 to 11 days ahead of Rakhi and Valentine's Day. This is a deadline aware planner, not a last minute panic buyer, and the page underserves them if it only optimises for urgency.

The Self-Gifter ▾

What they need to see: that buying for herself belongs here too, not a separate, apologetic corner of the site.

Mia's own brand positioning has been shifting toward self purchase, not just gifting to others. CaratLane's Self Rewards collection is the proven precedent for treating this as a first class path rather than an afterthought.

The Cold / Unfamiliar Visitor ▾

What they need to see: within seconds, that Mia is a legitimate, distinct jewellery brand, not assumed familiarity just because it sits under Tanishq.

Mia's own GA data shows a large "Awareness Only" cohort (15M+ DemandGen sessions, close to 0% CVR) with no prior brand context. Tata/Tanishq trust doesn't automatically transfer to "Mia" by name for someone who has never seen this specific sub-brand before.

The Consultative Decider ▾

What they need to see: an easy way to involve someone else in the decision, a sister, a partner, without screenshotting five product pages manually.

Referral and WhatsApp driven traffic converts at roughly 23x the site average on Mia's own property, the clearest signal available that this decision is frequently made in consultation, not solo.

Stage 1

Arrival & Trust

Header Quick-Links, Gifts for Her/Him, Card, Coins

The Late Planner

Why here: a direct route to the fastest formats (coins, gift card) for a visitor who doesn't have time for a guided flow, sitting one click above the Gift Finder, not buried under it.

Hero, "Gifts They'll Never Take Off"

Cold Visitor + Anxious Gifter

Why here: the headline promises an outcome (a gift that gets kept) to build immediate legitimacy for the Cold Visitor.

CTA logic: the two buttons split the audience right at the first viewport instead of routing everyone down one path. "Find A Gift In Under 30 Seconds" is the primary CTA and routes the undecided majority into the Gift Finder. The "30 seconds" in the copy itself is doing work, it answers the choice-overload concern before the visitor has even clicked, not just at the destination page. "Shop Gold Coins" is the secondary CTA, styled in gold, and exists because coins are Mia's #1 site search term, a visitor who already knows what they want should not need to complete a quiz to get there. One CTA serves the undecided, one serves the already-decided, both in the first viewport.

Stage 2

Finding The Gift

3 sections
▾

Gift Finder, Recipient x Occasion x Budget

Anxious Gifter + Early Planner + Self-Gifter

Why here: the highest leverage section on the page, serving three personas at once. Curation lowers the Anxious Gifter's choice overload, the occasion field serves the Early Planner's date awareness, and "Self" sits inside Recipient as a first class option for the Self-Gifter, with no added page length.

Added on top of that: guided quiz style finders are an established e-commerce pattern that converts better than flat browsing across categories generally, they narrow choice progressively instead of presenting everything at once. This is a category-agnostic best practice on top of the Mia-specific reasoning above, not a replacement for it.

Shop By Category, circular tiles into the product grid

Serves everyone equally

Why here: a category-first escape hatch for any persona that would rather browse than answer three questions. Placed below the Finder deliberately, so it never competes with it for the first viewport decision.

The product cards that appear once a category is selected (badge, rating, price, tax line) are folded into this same section rather than treated separately, they're the direct continuation of the category browse, not a second decision point.

Ring Sizer modal: triggered from Add to Cart on ring products, not placed with the other modals below, because sizing uncertainty is decided at the point of adding to cart, not later in the session. Offers a free ring sizer mailed in advance for a visitor who genuinely does not know the recipient's size, directly addressing Mia's top recorded complaint at the exact moment it would otherwise cause the visitor to abandon.

Gifts For Her / Gifts For Him, banner breaks inside the product grid

Serves everyone equally

Why here: this fills a real gap between the two options above it. The Gift Finder needs three fields answered before it shows anything, and Shop By Category requires knowing the product type already. This banner sits mid-grid as a single click, no-commitment path for a visitor who just wants "something for her" or "something for him" without either full quiz input or a specific category in mind.

Six Touches, wrap, note, on-time, insured, resize, lifetime

Anxious Gifter

Why here: this section is framed around the value of how the gift actually arrives and is opened, not only around de-risking the purchase decision. The velvet wrap, handwritten note, exact-date delivery and discreet insured packaging are all aimed at maximising the recipient's gratification at the moment of opening, the giver is buying that reaction as much as the piece itself. Free resize and lifetime exchange sit alongside as the practical safety net.
